Two ways in: select it, or draw a box around it

And when the words are a picture

Some text cannot be selected at all: a screenshot, a scanned page, a video, a game. There is nothing to highlight, so there is nothing to translate — until you draw a box around it instead.

Press the shortcut

⌘⇧E on a Mac, Ctrl+Shift+E on Windows and Linux. The screen dims, and the pointer becomes a crosshair.

Draw a box

Drag it around the words you want. Escape cancels, and nothing is captured until you let go.

Read the bubble

The words are read on your own computer, then translated. The bubble appears under the box, so it never covers what you were reading.

Prefer a different key?

Shift is the default because holding it already extends a selection, so translating stays one gesture. You can switch to Ctrl, Alt or Super (⌘ on macOS) in the app's settings — or choose Any selection to translate everything, the way it worked before. On some Linux Wayland sessions the key can't be detected; there the app translates every selection and says so.

Private by design

The text you select is the only thing that ever leaves your machine — and only when you select it.

🗂️

No translation history

bubbleTranslate doesn’t store your translation history. The recent list lives in memory for the session and is gone when you quit.

🔒

The licence server never sees your text

Your licence server never sees what you translate. It receives your licence key and a device id, and nothing else.

✂️

Only your selection is sent

Only the selected text is sent to the translation provider, together with the language to translate into.

By design, not by promise

No account. No sign-up.

The free version was built this way on purpose. There is no profile to create, no email to hand over, nothing to log in to — and data that is never asked for cannot be leaked, sold or subpoenaed.

No database in the app.

Your settings, your licence and your daily count are plain files on your own machine. Nothing about you is kept on a server of ours. Delete the files and nothing is left behind — they are yours, not ours.

The one time we learn anything is a Pro purchase. Paddle takes the payment, so your card details never reach us. The licence service keeps your email, the status and end date of your licence, and one device id per machine so that the three-machine limit can work. Not even your licence key is stored — only an irreversible hash of it. What you translate is never part of any of it.

Download binary
Make it executable and run it: 
chmod +x bubbleTranslate-linux-x86_64

Reading text off the screen (Ctrl+Shift+E) Ctrl+Shift+E reads text off the screen out of the box: a built-in reader is downloaded the first time you use it (12 MB). It only knows letters without accents, though — ç, ğ, ş, é, ü come out as plain c, g, s, e, u, and Cyrillic, Arabic or Chinese is not read at all, so a word missing its letters can translate as a different one. For it to read properly, install Tesseract, with a pack for each language you read from (eng, tur, deu, spa…); once it is there, it does the reading instead: 
# Ubuntu / Debian
sudo apt install tesseract-ocr tesseract-ocr-eng
# Fedora
sudo dnf install tesseract tesseract-langpack-eng
# Arch
sudo pacman -S tesseract tesseract-data-eng
Installed once, it stays through every update, and translating selected text needs none of it. Works on X11, Hyprland, sway, GNOME and KDE — on GNOME and KDE your desktop may ask once whether bubbleTranslate may take screenshots.

Installing on macOS — the first run

  1. Open the DMG and drag bubbleTranslate.app into Applications. Install it there — not run from the DMG or Downloads.
  2. Launch it once. macOS says it "could not verify" the app — that's Gatekeeper reacting to an app Apple hasn't notarized, not anything the app does. Dismiss it, open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll to Security, and click Open Anyway.
  3. Grant Accessibility: System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility, and switch bubbleTranslate on. This is how it reads what you select — a different permission from Open Anyway.
  4. Quit and relaunch. The Accessibility permission only takes effect on a fresh start, so quit bubbleTranslate (the globe in the menu bar → Quit) and open it again. Until you do, the window says "Not watching for selections."
  5. Done. Hold Shift and select text anywhere, and the bubble appears at your cursor.
  6. Reading text from a picture — only if you want it. ⌘⇧E reads a region of the screen: a photo, a video still, a scanned page. The first time you press it, macOS opens System Settings › Privacy & Security › Screen Recording with bubbleTranslate already in the list — switch it on, then quit and reopen the app. Like Accessibility, this permission only takes effect on a fresh launch, and until it is on the shortcut does nothing at all.

Still "Not watching" after relaunching? Remove bubbleTranslate from the Accessibility list (select it, click −), run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/bubbleTranslate.app, then add it back, switch it on, and relaunch. A leftover entry from an older build can silently block the new one.

Installing on Windows — the first run

  1. Unzip the download, then put bubbleTranslate.exe wherever you keep programs and double-click it. There is no installer — the zip holds one file, and it writes its settings to %APPDATA%\bubbleTranslate the first time it runs.
  2. Windows says "Windows protected your PC". That is SmartScreen reacting to a file it has not seen signed, not to anything the app does. Click More info, then Run anyway — once per machine, not once per launch.
  3. No permission to grant: Windows lets it read what you select straight away, so it starts watching the moment it opens.
  4. Find the globe. Windows 11 hides every new tray icon behind the ^ arrow next to the clock — drag it out onto the taskbar to keep it there. That icon reopens the window, and it is how you quit.
  5. Done. Hold Shift and select text anywhere, and the bubble appears at your cursor.

Nothing appears in one particular app? If that window is running as administrator, Windows hides it from every program that isn't — including this one — and there is no permission that changes it. Everywhere else, check that you are holding the key: Shift by default, and the window's Behaviour section is where you change it.

Frequently asked questions

Everything worth knowing before you install.

Is bubbleTranslate free?

Yes. You get 10 translations a day for free, on macOS, Windows and Linux, with no account or sign-up. If you need more, Pro removes the daily limit for $2/month or $20/year.

What do I get with Pro?

Unlimited translations on up to three machines, for $2/month or $20/year (the yearly plan is two months free). One licence key covers all your machines, and it supports the app's ongoing development.

How do I pay for Pro?

Monthly or yearly. Billing goes through Paddle, the merchant of record, so you are charged in your own currency. Taxes, if applicable, are calculated and shown by Paddle at checkout. You can cancel anytime.

Which systems does it run on?

macOS, Windows and Linux. The Windows build is one .exe that needs nothing installed and runs on Windows 10 and 11, including ARM machines. On Linux the download runs on any distribution with glibc 2.28 or newer — Ubuntu 20.04, Debian 10, Fedora, Arch and everything since; reading text off the screen works out of the box, and reads properly — accents and every alphabet — with Tesseract installed.

Does it need an internet connection?

Yes. Translations come from online providers, tried in turn until one answers. Only the text you select is sent, and only to translate it.

What permission does it need?

On macOS, Accessibility — that's how the app sees what you've selected, and it is asked for the first time you run it. Reading a region of the screen needs a second one, Screen Recording, and that is only asked for the first time you press the shortcut; if you never use it you are never asked. On Windows and on most X11 and Wayland sessions, selecting needs nothing extra; reading the screen uses what the system already provides.

Does it translate everything I select?

No — only selections you make with a key held down. Shift by default, so extending a selection and translating it are one gesture. You can switch the key to Ctrl, Alt or Super, or set it to translate every selection like before. Copying and ordinary highlighting are left alone.

How do I report a bug or suggest something?

From inside the app. Open the bubbleTranslate window and use Send feedback: something broken, something missing, or something that annoyed you. It goes out from your own mail — your mail app, Gmail or Outlook — and it is read by the person who wrote the app.
